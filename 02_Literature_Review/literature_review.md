# Literature Review Supporting Analysis

## 1. Information Security Risk Assessment

Information security risk assessment is used to identify and evaluate risks affecting information assets and systems. The reviewed studies use different frameworks and methods depending on the environment and research objective.

## 2. Risk Assessment Methods

The literature includes qualitative and quantitative approaches. Common techniques identified in the proposal include ISO/IEC 27005, ISO/IEC 27001, NIST, FMEA, AHP and risk matrices.

Qualitative approaches can be easier to apply but may depend heavily on expert judgement. Quantitative approaches provide measurable values but may require more data and assumptions.

## 3. Risk Scoring and Risk Matrices

Risk matrices provide a simple way to combine likelihood and impact. The proposed CRAT uses a controlled 1–5 likelihood scale and 1–5 impact scale.

The prototype uses:

**Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact**

The resulting score is mapped to four risk levels.

## 4. Risk Prioritization

Risk prioritization helps determine which identified risks should receive greater attention. For CRAT, multiple risks are ordered from the highest risk score to the lowest risk score.

## 5. Limitations Identified

The 43 reviewed studies reveal recurring limitations:

- Manual assessment and expert judgement.
- Subjective or inconsistent risk scoring.
- Dependence on predefined risk values.
- Limited automation and practical tool support.
- Limited practical testing.

## 6. Research Gap

Existing risk assessment approaches may be effective but can be complex, specialised, manual, or dependent on expert judgement. The research therefore proposes CRAT as a simple, measurable and practical prototype for assessing, classifying and prioritizing information-security risks in cloud environments.

The proposal evaluates CRAT using risk-score correctness, classification accuracy, prioritization agreement and processing time.
